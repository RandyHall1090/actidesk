## Source notes / confidence

- **Verified via primary vendor pricing page** (WebFetch of the vendor's own `/pricing` page): Trumpet, Storydoc, Qwilr, Aligned, Recapped, GetAccept, PandaDoc. High confidence.
- **Not independently confirmed on the vendor's own page in this pass** (search-engine-synthesized figures only): Vidyard (multiple consistent third-party sources agree on $59/$99), BombBomb (sources disagree — flagged above, don't quote as fact), Loom (consistent across sources, moderate confidence).
- **TMT Shockbox**: no standalone pricing exists to find — confirmed by checking TMT's FAQ page directly, which states price "varies" and routes to a sales call, plus the blog post on the Shock-and-Awe strategy, which only says the how-to lives on the members' Dashboard. The $1,997/mo and $3,990-then-$1,995/mo figures are for TMT's Producers Club / Accelerators Club coaching tiers (found via search, not a page I fetched directly) — these are coaching memberships that happen to include Shockbox access, not a Shockbox subscription. Entry-level TMT tier price is genuinely not public.
- **One data-quality catch worth flagging**: an initial WebFetch pass falsely reported "Recapped is shutting down 7/31/2026, not accepting new customers." A second fetch of the same page's raw text found no such notice anywhere on the page — this was a fabrication/misattribution by the fetch tool's summarizer (likely confused with a "Dooly is shutting down, switch to Recapped" competitor-comparison page it also saw). Recapped pricing above is from the clean re-fetch and should be treated as current and reliable.
- Storydoc pricing shows a real discrepancy between search-snippet figures ($40/$60 per user, uncertain source) and the vendor's own page ($19.80/$36, billed annually) — the report above uses the primary-source figure.

## What this means for Securafy

**Feature-fit reality check first:** Online Shock-and-Awe Portal's actual scope (one fixed template, one link per prospect, view/play tracking, no e-signature, no CRM sync, no AI content generation, no mutual-action-plan/task tooling) sits below every paid tier surveyed except each vendor's free/entry tier. It is closest in spirit to Trumpet's Free-to-Pro boundary, Aligned's Starter-to-Basic boundary, or Storydoc's Starter tier — not to Pro/Scale/Enterprise tiers, which all add CRM sync, AI, and e-sign that this product doesn't have.

**But the niche changes the calculus.** This isn't competing head-to-head with Trumpet/Aligned/Qwilr for the same buyer. It's aimed at MSP/MSSP sales teams who already know and buy into the exact "Shock-and-Awe" sales playbook — a narrow, high-affinity niche where the alternative isn't a competing SaaS tool, it's building the desk-scene graphic yourself in Canva/Photoshop or paying TMT $2-4k/month for a coaching membership that only includes this as one incidental resource. That's a much weaker substitute than a $35-45/seat horizontal tool, which supports pricing above the generic-tool floor despite the thinner feature set.

**Marginal cost is a non-factor in pricing (it just sets the floor).** Fixed Vercel/Supabase/Vimeo costs plus ~3% Stripe fee mean gross margin is high at almost any price point above ~$10-15/seat — so the ceiling should be set by willingness-to-pay/perceived value, not cost-plus math.

**Recommendation: $29-49/seat/month, anchor at $39/seat/month**, with an annual option at ~15-20% off (matches Trumpet/Aligned/Qwilr convention). Rationale:
- Undercuts or matches the cheapest *paid* per-seat tier in the category (Trumpet Pro $45, Qwilr Starter $35-49, Aligned Basic $35) — credible as "the specialized, cheaper alternative to generic sales-room tools," not a stretch given the narrower feature set.
- Meaningfully above Storydoc's $19.80 floor and above a "cheap tool" perception — the desk-scene visual concept is a genuine, non-commodity differentiator none of these competitors have, which justifies pricing above the pure feature-count floor.
- Stay clear of Recapped's flat per-org model (not applicable — you have per-seat billing already decided) and clear of GetAccept's 5-seat minimum (adds friction for small MSP sales teams of 1-3 reps who are the likely early adopters).
- 14-day trial matches the category norm (Qwilr, PandaDoc both use 14 days) — no need to differentiate on trial length.

**What this research did not cover:** actual willingness-to-pay data from Securafy's own prospect list (no survey/interview done here — this is desk research only), BombBomb's real current pricing (source conflict, not resolved), and Enterprise-tier custom quotes for GetAccept/Qwilr/Aligned/PandaDoc (all gated behind sales calls, genuinely unpriced). If Securafy wants sharper confidence on the $29 vs. $49 anchor decision, the fastest next step is a handful of MSP-owner conversations at whatever price sounds "obviously worth it" vs. "too cheap to be real" — not more desk research, since every public comparable in this niche is already accounted for above.
